# gdd: section 1 amendment

## 1. Summary
Section 1 (Overview) changes in five places:
- The pitch now names seven corporations and moves the campaign monitoring and netrun choice to the City Grid view with its zoom dive.
- Pillar 1 gains the wheel-geometry freedoms (any-tick pointers, variable slice sizes) and the rule that cards and Firmware shape alignment.
- Pillar 2 gains enrage, so a fight cannot stall, and the permadeath wording stays.
- Pillar 4 is clarified: wheel mechanics stay in combat, and the grid view and netrun are one zoomable space.
- The structure diagram in 1.3 is redrawn for the new node list, the Exploit buildings, the HQ netrun, the zoom and the 7 corporations.

Unchanged: the core premise, the three-layer progression, the deterministic raid pillar and the win and loss conditions (the loss condition is clarified).

Rulings that don't touch Section 1 (their changes belong in Sections 2–11): phase counts and thresholds, boss HP bands, parking, tier base values, enrage numbers, amount modes, status rules, the Parasite ring, corruption cost, slice-size tables, Firmware, satellites, listeners, locks, the defense trio, upgrade levels, rewards, station bonuses, shop prices, rim gates, Sync Strike details, node-type details, event mechanics, raid modifiers, G11 states and the Site-name removal. Section 1 gets no new rules text for these, only the pillar-level references below.

## 2. Amendments

**A1. In 1.1 Pitch, replace** "You lead an unseen rebel cell fighting a biotech megacorp that bricks the life-critical implants of anyone who misses a subscription payment." **with:**

> You lead an unseen rebel cell fighting the corporations that run a city's life-critical systems, starting with a biotech megacorp that bricks the implants of anyone who misses a subscription payment. There are seven corporations to take down, and each has its own enemies, hidden story, boss and unique HQ netrun.

**A2. In 1.1 Pitch, replace** "Operatives *jack in* to corporate systems on netruns (single roguelike runs, about 15 minutes each), where combat is a deterministic puzzle of reading, nudging, spinning and flipping wheels." **with:**

> Operatives *jack in* to corporate systems on netruns (single roguelike runs, about 15 minutes each; HQ netruns may run longer), where combat is a deterministic puzzle of reading, nudging, spinning and flipping wheels. Every enemy is a puzzle of its own: fights are built from short phases, each of which asks a new question, so a fight is solved, not ground down.

**A3. In 1.1 Pitch, replace** "Between runs you expand your network across a digital city and defend it from corporate retaliation raids, until you can breach the corporation's final server and plant the kill code." **with:**

> Between runs you watch over your network on the City Grid, a procedurally generated digital city, choose where to jack in next, and defend your territory from corporate retaliation raids, until you can breach the corporation's final server and plant the kill code.

**A4. In 1.2 Design Pillars, replace pillar 1** with:

> 1. **Precision is the currency of skill.** Alignment on the wheel is the core mechanic. Pointers may sit on any tick and slices vary in size, so how precisely a landing reads is part of every wheel's design. Cards, classes, Firmware and Daemons expand the space around alignment. **Hubs grant passives, never actions; every action resolves through alignment.**

**A5. In 1.2, replace pillar 2** with:

> 2. **Escalating, high-stakes campaign.** Difficulty ramps with tier and Heat, and fights escalate within themselves: enemies change in phases, and a turn-based enrage keeps any fight from stalling. Death is permanent for the operative and pushes the campaign toward defeat.

**A6. In 1.2, replace pillar 4** with:

> 4. **System boundaries.** Wheel mechanics exist only in combat. Raids are deterministic graph auto-battles with a planning phase. The City Grid view and the netrun are one connected space: the player zooms between them without changing screens, but the rules of each stay separate.

**A7. In 1.2, insert a new pillar 5** after pillar 4:

> 5. **Every enemy is designed, not assembled.** Each enemy, mini-boss and boss has its own authored answer to "what is the question this fight asks?". Fight length comes from the number of phases (normal 1, elite 2, mini-boss 2–3, HQ boss 4–5), not from the size of the health pool (see 2.11 and 11.6, as amended). Tier sets an enemy's starting values only (HP and damage); its mechanics scale from those.

**A8. In 1.3 Structure, replace the code block** with:

```
PROFILE (permanent): unlocks, ICE records
 └─ CAMPAIGN (one of 7 corporations): City Grid, network territory, Heat, Schematics, roster
     ├─ CITY GRID VIEW: monitor the campaign (Heat chip, "RAID AT n" tick), choose the
     │    next netrun; the zoom dive leads from the grid into the netrun
     ├─ HQ: recruit, station, spend Schematics, pick a netrun and an operative
     ├─ NETRUN (one operative, one netrun, ~15 min): map → combats, events, shops, rests,
     │    workshops, caches, Server Racks; the player may zoom out to the grid and back
     │    └─ may be interrupted by a raid interlude
     ├─ HQ NETRUN (one per corporation, unique mechanics, may run longer than 7 layers)
     └─ RAIDS: triggered by Heat thresholds and other events, mid-run or between runs
 Win: breach the final server with enough Exploits. Loss: home server integrity reaches 0.
```

**A9. In 1.3, insert after the code block** this paragraph:

> A netrun target is a typed location on the grid (Event, Normal Fight, Elite Fight, Mini-Boss Fight, Boss Fight and so on). Only the corporation, its HQ building (the HQ netrun) and its boss carry proper names. Each Exploit is an access key and has its own building on the grid (see 4.1 and 8, as amended). A cut-off part of the network stays powered unless a node on it is DOWN or TAKEN (see 3.3, as amended). Input scope for this version is mouse and keyboard, with gamepad support as in Section 9.

(Note: "gamepad support as in Section 9" conflicts with the ruling "text 1.0 mouse/keyboard scope"; see section 4 below.)

## 3. Contradictions with other sections
- **Header (Platform line):** "PC (mouse + keyboard, gamepad)" vs the 1.0 mouse/keyboard scope. The header and Section 9 must agree.
- **2.1:** the "6 slices of 5 ticks" rule and "evenly spaced" pointers must change for variable sizes (2–7 ticks, 6-slice cap lifted) and any-tick pointers. 2.4's tier offsets become sub-tick quality maps.
- **2.5/2.6:** add block-nudge/respin locks; 2.6 BLOCK / SHIELD / SANDBOX naming and the new SANDBOX rule (negates a whole attack). 2.7: satellites are fixed, can replace one another, and pierce does not bypass SANDBOX.
- **2.9:** CORRUPTED is a double-RAM debuff and no longer deals damage. ×N stacks, OUTPUT_DOWN and TIER_DOWN are new statuses, and the Parasite ring is new.
- **2.11:** is no longer "experimental"; it needs phases for all classes, parking, enrage and boss buffs and Exploits.
- **3.3 / G11:** DOWN is a source for no one, TAKEN is corporation territory, and neither can launch netruns. Cut-off nodes stay powered.
- **4.1:** "30–40 Sites" and the Site names are replaced by typed Sites and the new procedural city. 4.2 needs the node list (Rest, Workshop, Cache; no Intel, Relay or Ambush nodes). 4.2 also needs the Heat chip and "RAID AT n" tick.
- **4.2:** "7 layers" conflicts with HQ runs that may be longer.
- **5.2:** station bonuses are unique per class. 5.3 and 6 need Level I–IV upgrades. 6.1 needs the tick-quality Firmware.
- **7:** raid modifiers R1–R4 are all added.
- **8.3–8.5:** the corporation list becomes seven (Aurelian Concord, Tethys Deep). Boss names follow the placeholder names from 11.7. Sync Strike appears in 8.5 and the DISPATCH finale.
- **11.2:** shop wheel prices of 100/80/100 and a nudge bought or granted by a Daemon.

## 4. Not covered / needs a ruling
- Whether "single roguelike runs, about 15 minutes each" still holds as the pitch figure, given HQ runs that may be longer. A1–A3 hedge with "HQ netruns may run longer".
- Whether gamepad stays in scope. The brief says 1.0 is mouse/keyboard; the header currently lists gamepad. A9 defers to Section 9, which needs a ruling.
- Whether the pitch should name Aurelian Concord and Tethys Deep or stay generic. A1 stays generic (D2: names are low effort).
