# gdd: section 4 amendment

## 1. Summary
Section 4 changes in five areas.
- **City Grid:** it is generated procedurally, Sites are identified by type and not by name, and Exploits become access keys that each have a building.
- **Node types:** the netrun map gains Rest, Workshop and Cache nodes, and the HQ netrun may run longer than 7 layers.
- **Fights:** fight length is set by phases, with HP set by tier band. This touches the node table, the pacing line and the Rack text.
- **Heat and raids:** the map shows a Heat chip and a "RAID AT n" tick; R1–R4 raid modifiers are added; DOWN and TAKEN rules are clarified.
- **Views:** the grid view and the netrun are joined by a zoom transition.

The locked 8-run minimum path, the Heat thresholds and bands, the death and completion rules, and 4.5 (Leaving a Run) are unchanged. Raid mechanics proper stay in section 7.

## 2. Amendments

**A1. In 4.1 (City Grid), replace "30–40 **Sites** per corporation. Each netrun targets one Site; cleared Sites are used up." with:**

- The City Grid is generated procedurally from the campaign seed: a coast, districts and terrain, laid out as a planner city. It is not a fixed layout. Old campaign saves are dropped.
- Each corporation's Grid holds 30–40 **Sites** (placeholder). Each netrun targets one Site; cleared Sites are used up.
- A Site has no name. It is identified by its type: Event, Normal Fight, Elite Fight, Mini-Boss Fight, Boss Fight, and so on. Only the corporation, its HQ building (the HQ netrun) and its boss have names.

**A2. In 4.1, replace "Each T2 Site holds at most one Exploit; each Exploit type sits at a different Site." with:**

Each T2 Site holds at most one Exploit; each Exploit type sits at a different Site. An Exploit is an **access key**. Each Exploit has its own special building on the Grid, which the player unlocks by holding the key. Which Exploits a Grid offers is a subset of the corporation's Exploit pool (5 per Grid, placeholder), so not every boss buff can be countered in every campaign (see 8.3 and the boss rules).

**A3. In 4.1, replace "**Cross-links** between Sites start locked; Intel Exploits or objectives open them. Opened links are routes for raids too." with:**

**Cross-links** between Sites start locked; Exploits or objectives open them. Opened links are routes for raids too.

**A4. In 4.1, insert after the Heat objective Sites bullet:**

- **Territory states.** A DOWN node is a source for no one. A TAKEN node is corporation territory. Neither can launch netruns. Nodes cut off behind a TAKEN or DOWN chokepoint stay powered; they are not closed down. A netrun may launch from any owned node to any unowned Site.
- **Grid-view nodes.** Safehouse and Heat Sink are Grid-view nodes. They are built on the City Grid and never appear inside a netrun.

**A5. Insert a new subsection 4.1b "Grid View and Netrun":**

1. The campaign is monitored and the next netrun chosen in the Grid view.
2. The storyboard starts in the full Grid view. JACK IN plays a zoom dive into the netrun.
3. During a netrun the player may zoom out to the Grid view and back in to the netrun at will, using the same zoom transition. There is no separate Grid-view toggle page.
4. The Grid view shows a **Heat chip** and, when a raid is queued, a **"RAID AT n"** tick showing the Heat value that fires it.

**A6. In 4.2 (Netrun Map), replace "**7 layers**, 2–4 nodes each, branching paths." with:**

- A standard netrun has **7 layers** (placeholder), 2–4 nodes each, branching paths. The whole graph is drawn in a muted style. Hover, or the map legend highlight, brings it to a full active state with all information. Nodes beyond the next layer are never hidden.
- An **HQ netrun** may run longer than 7 layers. Each corporation's HQ netrun is unique, with its own mechanics.

**A7. In 4.2, replace the node table with:**

| Node | Content | Heat |
|---|---|---|
| Router | Standard combat (1 phase, 3–4 turns) | 0 |
| Elite Router | Elite combat (2 phases, 6–8 turns) | +1 |
| Terminal | Event (Section 8.7 voices) | 0 |
| Mainframe | Shop (Cycles) | 0 |
| Rest | Heal, or swap Firmware between sockets | 0 |
| Workshop | Work on slices and cards (details in the upgrade rules) | 0 |
| Cache | An event mini-puzzle with a reward | 0 |
| Server Rack | Elite-strength combat + banking | per 11.5 table |

The map has no Intel, Relay or Ambush nodes. A Relay or shortcut may appear as a unique feature of a specific netrun, such as an HQ. An ambush may appear as an event option, not as a node.

**A8. In 4.2, replace "- Guarantees: layer 1 all Routers; at least one Mainframe in layers 3–5; about 1 Elite per layer in layers 3–6; Terminals ≈ 25% of remaining nodes." with:**

- Guarantees: layer 1 all Routers; at least one Mainframe in layers 3–5; about 1 Elite per layer in layers 3–6; Terminals ≈ 25% of remaining nodes; at least one Rest in layers 3–6 (placeholder). Workshop and Cache nodes replace a share of the remaining Terminals (placeholder).

**A9. In 4.2, replace "Server Racks at layer 4 (optional, one node among others) and layer 7 (the single final node)... On T2+ targets, the final Rack holds the Site's Exploit." with:**

- **Server Racks** at layer 4 (optional, one node among others) and at the final layer (the single final node). Capturing a Rack banks its Schematics immediately (and banks Armory assets).
- The final Rack holds a **mini-boss** (2–3 phases, 6–12 turns). On T2+ targets it holds the Site's Exploit. The Site's boss is fought only at the HQ netrun.
- A Rack may offer a **flash** reward: upgrade or swap a slice, or a stronger reward than a normal node.

**A10. In 4.2, replace "- Pacing: about 5 combats of roughly 2 minutes plus events ≈ 15 minutes." with:**

- Pacing: about 5 combats of 3–4 turns (normal) or 6–8 turns (elite), plus events ≈ 15 minutes. Fight length is set by phases (normal 1, elite 2, mini-boss 2–3, HQ boss 4–5, each 3–4 turns), not by HP alone. Fight HP comes from the tier and the fight class (`hp_by_tier`, boss HP bands), and tier sets base values only.
- What rewards a node offers (cards, Firmware, Schematics, slice flash) is defined in a data-driven rewards list: each row names a kind, a pool, a count, a chance and conditions (node type, elite, Rack, tier, layer).

**A11. Insert a new subsection 4.2b "Events":**

1. Terminal events are added first; consolidation and pruning come later.
2. An event choice may alter a future fight or the paths available (a next-combat override, a map edit), or start an impromptu fight.
3. Some events use an **event wheel**: outcome slices on a wheel, with a nudge budget and a cost for extra nudges.
4. Events may also appear in the HQ or raid view, and a won raid may show a reward screen.

**A12. In 4.2, replace "**Death:** the operative is permanently lost..." — no change to text; add after the Completion bullet:**

A Rest node's Firmware swap moves chips between sockets and never destroys a chip. A Firmware that would replace a socketed chip asks for confirmation first, naming the chip it destroys.

**A13. In 4.3 (Heat), after the threshold table, insert:**

The map shows the current Heat as a chip. When a threshold raid is queued, a "RAID AT n" tick marks the Heat value that fires it. Heat bands are unchanged.

**A14. In 4.4 (Raid Triggers & Timing), replace "**Raid rewards are set by the trigger; raid strength scales with current Heat**, so low-Heat raids are profitable." with:**

**Raid rewards are set by the trigger; raid strength scales with current Heat**, so low-Heat raids are profitable. Raids may use these modifiers (all four in one raid design, placeholders):
1. **EXPOSED spotlight:** one node takes extra damage. The player may move the spotlight along a link for Heat or Schematics.
2. **Encrypted routes:** at high Heat a threat's path is hidden until decrypted for a cost.
3. **Vehicle types:** LANDER enters inside the network; FLYING ignores ICE Lock and Tar Pit; HEAVY halves Turret damage.
4. **Heat-scaled second wave with an ultimatum:** the player pays Schematics or Heat to cancel it, or takes it for the reward. This is the Schematics sink.

## 3. Contradictions with other sections
- **§2.10/§2.11, 2.1, 2.7:** pointers on any tick, variable slice sizes, fixed satellites and phase/enrage rules belong to the combat amendment. A4's "node's boss" wording assumes the phase classes.
- **§3.3:** TAKEN/DOWN launch rules and "stays powered" must match A4.
- **§5.3:** Rank unlocks T4 access and "Netrun tier access" must agree with HP-by-tier fights.
- **§8.3/8.4b–d:** boss names, Exploit effects (Intel/Breach/Virus as buff counters) and the seven corporations (Aurelian Concord, Tethys Deep) need adding. The "Exploit-based Central Server" in the Changes list must match the boss-buff model.
- **§9:** the Grid/netrun zoom, the less intrusive graph look and the Heat chip must be listed. **§11.2 and 11.5:** the shop wheel prices (100/80/100), Heat per node type, the `min_exploits_for_breach = 3` rule and HQ netrun length need aligning. **§7:** raid modifiers R1–R4 and reward screens must be mirrored.
- **Section 1.3 structure diagram:** "NETRUN ... map → combats, events, shops, Server Racks" must add the new node types.
