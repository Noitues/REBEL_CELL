# gdd: section 8 amendment

## 1. Summary
- Section 8 grows from five corporations to seven. Aurelian Concord and Tethys Deep are added as full playable corporations (D1). Names are placeholders (D2).
- The boss names in 8.3 to 8.4d are updated to the GDD 11.7 names. The Exploit list in each corporation entry is reframed: an access key is an Exploit, and each Exploit has its own building (D5).
- Enemy descriptions are aligned with the new status vocabulary: Citations become a status plus a Parasite-ring segment, Dose becomes a RAM debuff, and so on.
- 8.5 (REBEL_CELL) and 8.2 (DISPATCH) are amended for the Sync Strike finale. REBEL_CELL's unlock condition is amended for seven corporations.
- 8.4 covers the story paths of the two new corporations. The Site-name removal is stated here.
- A new 8.7 states that Sites are named by type. A new 8.8 collects the narrative hooks of the new mechanics (Parasites, boss buffs, events that alter future fights).
- Not changed: the tone map, the slang lexicon, the DISPATCH clue list, the delivery list (a few additions only), and the story-path structure rule.

"### 8.7 Names
A Site has no name of its own. A Site is shown as its type: Event, Normal Fight, Elite Fight, Mini-Boss Fight, Boss Fight, Rest, Workshop, Cache and so on. Only three things carry names: the corporation, its HQ building (the HQ netrun) and the HQ boss. Story beats refer to Sites by type and position, never by a proper name. Names are placeholders until the enemy design passes settle (D2).

### 8.8 Narrative of the New Systems
1. **Parasites and statuses.** Narratively, a Parasite is a hostile program that rides your wheel (a Package, a Citation, a latched ring segment). Statuses are degradations of your Firmware. Text uses these words consistently.
2. **Boss buffs and Exploits.** A boss buff is a corporate security measure; the Exploit that turns it off is a piece of stolen access. Because a Grid draws only a subset, each story path names which Exploit the finale 'relies on' only as flavor; it never promises counterability.
3. **Events that change the run.** A Terminal event may alter a future fight or the available paths, start an impromptu fight, or open an event wheel. Event text states the risk in plain terms before the choice. Decision events are added first; consolidating and pruning the existing events comes later.
4. **Grid view and netrun.** The story is delivered in the full grid view (the storyboard starts there). After JACK IN, a zoom takes the player into the netrun, and the player can zoom out to the grid view and back at will, so DISPATCH and raid warnings can reach the player mid-run (the map shows a Heat chip and a 'RAID AT n' tick).
5. **Rest, Workshop, Cache.** These netrun nodes are the narrative's quiet beats (a safe corner of the corporate system). Safehouse and Heat Sink are grid-view nodes, not netrun nodes. There are no Intel, Relay or Ambush nodes."
