# o_e_ransom_collage: ransom-note graphic collage

**Medium.** The Cell's voice is cut out of other people's paper. Every verb is assembled one letter at a time, and each letter is cut from a different "magazine": a tile or a die-cut glyph in a different typeface, size and tilt. The letters sit on sharp angular slabs, starbursts, slashes and arrow wedges. Notes are typewriter strips cut from a page. The crew photo is a two-plate halftone print (black at 45° over a misregistered pink plate at 15°) in a thick, angular black frame.

**Palette (strict).** Cell pink `#FF2684`, ink black `#0D0B0E`, paper white `#F6F2E8` and toxic yellow `#DCFF00`. No other hues. The only extras are dot screens in those inks. The Cv2 base is lightly desaturated (0.85) and darkened under each piece with a blurred silhouette.

**Lettering.** The letter pool uses 12 heavy display families. Stencil and ultra-condensed faces were dropped because they break readability. Consecutive letters never repeat a font or a paper colour. Numerals use a smaller, clearer pool. Machine words (EXECUTE, PURCHASE, EXIT) stay in the base UI font, washed grey, with a pink cut strip struck through them. The human word is pasted over the machine word.

**Why it reads AAA.** It has one rigid rule set: four papers, a hard offset shadow on every piece and razor-cut polygon edges with a 1 px lit rim. Black slabs cast a *pink* hard shadow, and light pieces cast a black one. This gives real stacked-paper depth on a dark base. Faint paper grain and a gloss sweep add material response. The flat, faceted, rain-lit Cv2 world never has offset shadows or halftone, so the overlay reads instantly as a different material on top. Each screen has 4–6 marks.

**Godot 2D build.** Each letter is a `Sprite2D` (or `TextureRect`) from a pre-baked atlas: per font × scheme × glyph, cut masks included. Alternatively, glyphs can be rendered at runtime into a `SubViewport` and masked by a `Polygon2D` with a seeded jag. The hard shadow is the same texture drawn as a child offset by (6, 8) with `modulate` set to the shadow ink. A soft ambient layer could use a cheap blur shader. Halftone is a `CanvasItem` shader: a rotated grid, `step(length(fract(uv*freq)-0.5), r)`, with r taken from a gradient or a texture. Starbursts and slashes are `Polygon2D` with seeded vertices. All layouts take a `RngService` stream, so a verb always cuts the same way for a given seed. For motion, one `Tween` per letter handles the snap-in: 60 ms stagger, scale 1.4→1.0, rotation +14°→0, `TRANS_BACK`, with the shadow trailing by one frame. Idle is a stepped 8 fps jitter of ±2 px and ±2°. Exit scatters the pieces outward with a 30 ms stagger. A `scale.x` flip through 0 swaps in the paper back, and the slab drops last.

**Risks.** The look sits close to a famous stylish-collage UI genre, so it must keep its own motifs: pink, yellow and black only, typewriter strips, the halftone ID print and broken-ring target marks. Avoid red, black and white with calling-card shapes. Mixed fonts can hurt legibility at small sizes. Keep the letter pool curated, and use a minimum size of about 34 px at 1080p. It is easy to overdo, so the 4–6 marks per screen rule must be held. Generated letter atlases will be large, which favours runtime cutting with a shader mask. The procedural halftone portrait is a stand-in, and real crew art should be fed through the same two-plate print.
