# Street graffiti: real-world reference notes (2026-10-21)

Short notes from a web pass, turned into rules for the procedural atlas (`scripts/graf_atlas.py`) and the facade overlay.

## What real writers paint, and where
| Form | What it is | Our rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Tag** | The writer's name, one colour, fast, hand script. Tokyo tags show calligraphy and kana / kanji influence. | Hand-script tags in Latin and in kana / kanji, over everything, the last layer. |
| **Throw-up** | Two or three colours, quick bubble or block letters, repeated. Classic: chrome (silver) fill + black outline. | Chrome and two-colour throw-ups at street level, repeated names. |
| **Piece / burner** | Big, many colours: fill fades, highlights, 3D extrude, outline, overspray halo, often a character beside it. | Bubble pieces with extrude, two outlines, gradient fill, highlights, drips; skull characters. |
| **Wildstyle** | Interlocking letters with arrows and spikes, hard to read. | The `wild` factor: tighter overlap, rotation, arrows. |
| **Blockbuster / roller** | Huge square letters, two colours, made to read from far away; fills a whole wall. | The **heaven layer**: blockbuster words high on the walls and parapets. |
| **Heaven spot** | Work placed somewhere very hard to reach (rooftops, high walls); prestige comes from the placement. | Blockbusters and big tags in a band near the tops of towers, sparse, readable from the city camera. |
| **Buff** | Grey / off-colour paint-outs over removed graffiti, repainted over again. | Buffed rectangles in the oldest layer. |
| **Wheat-paste / stickers** | Posters and slaps on poles, boxes, signs. | Poster clusters with kana / kanji headlines. |

Tokyo specifics: graffiti is densest in Shibuya, Harajuku and Shinjuku, with Koenji and Shimokitazawa heavy in tags and stickers.
It sits on **shop shutters** (whole shutters tagged or painted), electrical boxes, the backs of signs, railings and footbridges;
bombers (e.g. the Route 246 crews) favour fast tags and throw-ups over pieces. Our brief asks for more than real Tokyo has
("graffiti on most building surfaces"), so we take Tokyo's *forms* and placements and push the density.

## Rules taken into the look
1. **Height zones.** Street band (ground to about 3 floors): dense pieces, throw-ups, posters and tags. Above it: buff patches,
   sparse tags and throw-ups. Near the tops: the heaven layer (blockbusters, big roller tags).
2. **Paint, not light.** Graffiti is paint: it takes the street's neon light. Fluoro fills get a small self-glow (as under the
   UV and neon spill of a night street) so they pop; black outlines and posters never glow.
3. **Wear.** Patchy paint loss, overspray speckle, grime; old pieces faded and partly buffed; new over old.
