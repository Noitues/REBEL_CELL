# Review: Raid lab (city raid), batch of scrambled / planned / breach captures

**Verdict:** The rules engine tells the truth. Every route, response and intensity shows its outcome before you commit, and HOME, decay and Heat carry forward across the leg. The board does not yet tell that truth on its own. The hunter is close to invisible, the warnings arrive one raid too late, and because everything is pre-solved, the playout mostly confirms what the panel already said. **34/50**, which places it between FTL and Kingdom Rush.

> Scope: I judged only the attached contact sheets (scrambled 000–028, planned 000–028, breach 000–030) and the lab README. The README calls this lab "L8"; this file is named L12. I assume they are the same build. Stills cannot show motion, so S is judged from the beat sequence only.

## What the scrambled run conveyed (read first, images only)

**Read from images before opening the README:**

- **The board:**
  - It is an isometric city strip with an amber stacked HQ on the left (ours, by its pips in the HUD) and a cobalt tower on the right that emits beams.
  - Three named rows run between them (scrambled EYRCE / DYA / ZTOZI) and four lettered columns (B, C, G, D).
- **The leg structure:**
  - There are three rounds: the `1/3 → 2/3 → 3/3` counter survives scrambling.
  - Each round opens with yellow `+` rings carrying numbers (6, 10, 12), which I read as claimable districts with values. One of them has a key glyph, which I read as some bonus (scrambled/010).
- **Type matching:** beams come in three colors and shapes (cyan triangle, magenta square, orange circle). They match the legend glyphs and the sentry glyphs, so "a cyan sentry stops a cyan beam" is guessable from scrambled/001–/002.
- **Forecast:** small hearts and broken hearts stack at our HQ per row, and a skull with a red ring appears when it goes badly (scrambled/002, /012).
  - The panel rows carry matching green, amber and red tags.
  - These tags **match the aftermath icon**: the orange row's red X in scrambled/002 becomes the red X in /007. So "the panel predicts the result" is decodable from color alone.
- **Outcomes:**
  - The HQ pip strip turns one pip red after round 1, and a floating red `-1` with a red ring sits on the HQ (scrambled/006–/007).
  - Round 2 ends with a green heart (/017) and round 3 with an amber broken heart (/027).
  - The record (/028) shows X, heart and broken heart with a final number of 33.
- **Mid-round beats:** a row of speed buttons (1, 2, 4, plus a fourth) appears. Green-yellow rings around our sentries (/005, /015, /025) suggest "you may act on these now".
- **Between rounds:**
  - Three cards carry 1, 2 and 3 flames with different numbers, which I read as a risk-vs-reward pick.
  - A row of buyable sentry glyphs appears, and a wrench-style action is applied to a district (/009).
- **HUD:** the orange top bar climbs 44 → 56 → 50 → 68 → 70, with two tick marks around 50 and 75.

**What it did NOT convey without words:**

- **Which beam was "the real one".** The launch list's dimmed `?` row (scrambled/003, /013, /023) hints at a hidden item, but the board never marks the three candidates as candidates during launch.
- **What the hunter is.** The `sealed_hunter` frames (/006, /016, /026) look almost identical to the `reveal` frames.
- **What the flame cards cost.** I could see bigger numbers, but not that Heat adds beams at 50 and 75 and is charged at scoring.
- **Why round 1 was lost.** It was unsavable at response, since all three response rows were red (/005), but nothing on the board says "you were already beaten when you launched".
- **Repair vs decay.** That an unrepaired district is lost next round is invisible here; this run never showed a decay beat.
- **The forecast numbers** ("1 hit, 2 blocks…") and the difference between a "block" and a "hit".

**After the README:** the scrambled leg gambled every raid on the 1-in-3 sealed draw.

## Findings

### P1: fix before judging the concept further

1. **The hunter has almost no board presence, yet showing it is the lab's stated purpose.**
   - `planned/004_r1_reveal` vs `planned/006_r1_sealed_hunter` differ by a faint dashed mark. `scrambled/014` vs `/016` are near-identical. In `breach/006` and `/016` the hunter is a few small numbered circles near column 2.
   - The forecast board (`planned/002`, `breach/012`) draws routes but not the hunter's street. The plan panel only says "the street of the first touch" (`planned/001`, `breach/011`).
   - Without words, "hunter" does not exist (N, W).
2. **Launch friction fires only at certain death; the earlier near-certain losses get no gate.**
   - `breach/002`: every route is forecast DAMAGED with HOME at 3/3. The run launches freely.
   - `breach/012`: every route DAMAGED or BATTERED with HOME at 2/3. Still no gate, and the result is HOME 1/3 (`breach/017`).
   - Only `breach/024` (all BREACHED) shows "Every route breaks HOME… change it?" with a concrete fix: "DRONE on MID 1, NETRUNNER on SOU 1, ghost on HOME".
   - That fix existed in some form two raids earlier, and the campaign was lost by not being told (P, D).
3. **"a response changes this" is printed on every forecast row, including rows where no response helps.**
   - `breach/002` shows it on all three DAMAGED rows; `breach/005` then offers three responses, all DAMAGED.
   - The same happens in `breach/023` → `/027` (all BREACHED). In `planned/022` → `/025` it happened to be true (JAM turned BATTERED into HOLDS).
   - A boilerplate promise erodes the otherwise excellent P (P, W).

### P2: holds the score down

4. **The panel carries the rules, and it is hard to read.**
   - Outcome numbers ("2 hit, 1 block, HOME -1 HOME 2/3") are about 8px grey on navy (`planned/011`, `breach/022`).
   - The response row text overruns its tag in `planned/025` ("DEPLOY (2 way[s]" collides with BATTERED).
   - Decay lines are truncated in `breach/020` and `/021` ("(its DRONE sentry ret…").
   - The board, the readable part, holds only a fraction of this information (V, N).
5. **Response outcomes are pre-labeled, so the playout after the response is a confirmation, not a beat.**
   - `planned/015` shows `DEPLOY DRONE > NOR 0 ♥ HOLDS` before the player picks it.
   - The following `sealed_hunter` (`planned/016`) and `aftermath` (`/017`) frames can only agree. The only live unknown in a raid is the sealed draw at `reveal` (S).
6. **Heat's two thresholds and its end cost are text-only.**
   - The bar's `50 +1 / 75 +2` ticks are tiny (every HUD), and the extra beams appear as "EXTRA 1 off (at Heat 50)" in the plan text (`planned/001`).
   - In `planned/028`, "Heat above 44: −26" outweighs the whole bank (+15). It is the biggest number in the score and the least visible on the board (N, D).
7. **Sealed vs known beams are styled the same once revealed.**
   - In `planned/013` → `/014` the sealed NORTH drone beam lights up like the known beams.
   - Neither the scrambled reader nor the stills can tell which beam was the hidden one after the fact (N).
8. **HOME has no visible repair path.**
   - `breach/018` lists three DOWN districts but no HOME option, while a red banner says "Next HOME hit ends the campaign".
   - This may be intended, but then the 3-pip HOME bar should say "no repair" on the board. Right now three DAMAGED raids end a campaign with no in-between lever (C is strong; W suffers).

### P3: polish

9. A stale footer hint, "Every route breaks HOME with this plan: change it?", persists through `breach/025` to `/030`, including the record screen.
10. The ghost of the "SOUTH" label ("TH") bleeds through the record cards in `planned/028` and `breach/030`.
11. `breach/030`'s right panel is near-empty ("A breach leaves no score."). This is a missed chance to show *which* raid made the loss inevitable (breach/012).
12. The lab's naming is inconsistent: the README says L8, the review batch says L12.

## The 3 changes that would raise the score most

1. **Put the hunter on the board as a first-class threat (N +1, W +1, S +0.5).**
   - Draw its street as a distinct hound lane (dashed, pawprint glyph) in plan and forecast, starting at the predicted first-touch cell.
   - Give it its own row in the forecast panel and its own animated run after the response.
   - This fixes P1-1 and gives the post-response playout something to show.
2. **Graduated, honest launch friction (P +1, D +0.5).**
   - Gate the launch whenever HOME drops on **every** candidate, or whenever HOME is at 1. Do not wait for "every route BREACHED".
   - Always show the "One fix" line when one exists.
   - Replace the boilerplate "a response changes this" with "best response: JAM → HOLDS" or "no response saves this route".
   - This turns the naive line (breach/002, /012) into a visible decision instead of a silent loss.
3. **A wordless outcome-and-cost layer on the board (N +1.5, V +0.5).**
   - Move the per-route tier icons from the HQ stack onto each lane's entry, and keep them through launch.
   - Mark the three sealed candidates with a shared "?" glyph that collapses on reveal.
   - Show Heat thresholds as extra-beam ghosts on the corp tower.
   - Show DOWN districts with a countdown glyph that becomes TAKEN.
   - Together with (1), this takes N from 5 to about 7.

Combined, these bring the lab to roughly **38–39/50**, level with Invisible, Inc.

## Honest note: rules ceiling or presentation ceiling?

- **Mostly presentation for W, N and V.** The engine already computes everything a player needs: per-route outcomes, response outcomes, a fix, and the decay list. That information sits in a cramped panel instead of on the board, and points 1 and 3 above are paint, not rules.
- **Rules ceiling on D and S.**
  - A 3-raid leg with at most 2 sentries, one capture per raid and an enumerated response menu is a small, fully forecast puzzle. The lab can print a solver's "One fix" (breach/024), and the greedy auto line held 3/3 by always picking LOW and patching with a reserve or JAM (planned/028).
  - To climb toward ITB's 45, the leg needs choices that interact across raids. Examples: the hunter's street depending on *your* placement; captured districts changing route geometry; exploits that alter which routes can be sealed.
  - S stays near 5 by design as long as responses are pre-labeled. That is the ITB trade, and it is acceptable, but then D has to carry the depth.
- **C and P are already near their ceiling.** Protect them while fixing the rest.
