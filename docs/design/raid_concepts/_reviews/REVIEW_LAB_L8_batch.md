# Review: Raid lab L8, the city raid (batch: scrambled / planned / breach)

**Verdict:** The rules read well and are close to Into the Breach on prediction. The game tells you almost exactly what each sealed route will do before you launch. That same forecast also solves the response for you, and the board cannot carry the outcome without words. The lab is a strong forecasting skeleton with a thin decision layer and a text-dependent presentation.

## Placement

**33/50 places the lab level with Kingdom Rush (33), above Teamfight Tactics (31) and below FTL (35).**

I only have the anchors' totals and their S/V, not their per-axis scores, so the comparisons below are inferred.

- **Against Kingdom Rush:**
  - The lab wins on P and C: it forecasts exact outcomes, and HOME damage is permanent and campaign-ending.
  - It loses on N, on S (5 vs 8) and on V (6 vs 8). Kingdom Rush reads entirely from the board, and this lab does not.
- **Against FTL:**
  - The lab wins clearly on P, since FTL is a dice game by comparison.
  - It loses on D (no crew/system layering) and on S (5 vs 8).
- **Against TFT:**
  - The lab is ahead on P and W.
  - It is behind on S and V.
- **Against Into the Breach (45):**
  - The lab shares its forecast-first DNA and its low S.
  - The 12-point gap is mostly N (ITB draws every outcome on the tiles) and D (ITB's responses are not pre-solved).

## The scrambled run: what images alone conveyed (written before reading the README)

**Understood from scrambled/000–028 alone:**
- **The setting.** A three-lane isometric city sits between an amber base on the left (mine) and a cobalt tower on the right (the enemy). A segmented orange bar at the top has ticks at "50 +1" and "75 +2". I read it as a threat meter that adds something at 50 and more at 75. It reads 44 → 50 → 68 → 70 across the run.
- **Capture** (scrambled/000, /010, /020). Big yellow discs with numbers (6/10/12) are claimable cells, and the number is their worth. A key icon (scrambled/010) marks a special cell.
- **Placement** (scrambled/001). I place three towers in three shapes and colors: cyan triangle, magenta square, orange circle. Dashed colored lines run from the cobalt tower toward my side. The color match suggests each tower answers its own color, but nothing proves that.
- **Pre-launch report** (scrambled/002). There is one card per colored lane, each with two sub-rows (a default and an alternative) and an identical blue tag on every row. I read "all fine."
- **Launch** (scrambled/003, /004, /006).
  - One row is a "?" (unknown) that later resolves to a colored lane.
  - The "1r/2r/4r" speed buttons are obvious.
  - Rows flip from a grey tag to an orange tag, which reads as now/done.
- **Response** (scrambled/005, /025). A list of alternatives appears, each with a tag. In raid 3 every row is orange except one blue row, and the run picks the blue one.
- **Aftermath** (scrambled/007). A big blue word appears, the meter does not change, and the three amber pips stay full. I read "survived."
- **Between raids** (scrambled/008, /009, /018, /019).
  - Three cards trade meter rise against money, and the projected meter and money in the top bar update live.
  - In raid 2 a "-6" appears next to a cell name, which reads as a repair cost.
- **Record** (scrambled/028). Three framed rows, one per raid, all with the blue word, and a ledger that sums to 58.

**Not conveyed by images alone:**
- **What the "+1/+2" meter ticks add.** I only guessed "more threats."
- **What the ghost (dashed circle) does.** It was invisible as a concept.
- **The hunter.** It is just another row and a white bloom blob (scrambled/006, /016). I could not tell it was a separate threat, or where it would run.
- **The severity scale.**
  - The run only ever showed blue and orange, so I never learned there is a red tier, or that HOME can be lost.
  - I inferred blue = good only because the pips did not change.
  - Blue is also the enemy HQ's color, which works against that reading.
- **Why the meter matters for score.** The ledger's last negative line is unreadable without words.
- **Whether sentries strictly block only their own type.** The colors suggest it; nothing confirms it.

**Net:** I recovered the loop (claim → place → forecast → reveal → respond → spend). I did not recover the stakes (HOME loss), the hunter, the ghost, or what Heat does. This supports N = 5.

## Findings

### P1

1. **The forecast's BEST row solves the response before launch.**
   - In planned/022, every route's BEST is "JAM the hunter," HOLDS against BATTERED. Planned/025 then offers 8 response rows, 7 BATTERED and 1 HOLDS, the same answer.
   - In planned/002 and planned/012, every route already HOLDS on HOLD, so the reveal (planned/004, planned/014) changes nothing.
   - The reveal-and-respond beat, the mode's signature, is therefore either a lookup or a no-op. This costs D and S.
2. **Outcome severity depends on words, and its colors fight the palette.**
   - HOLDS is pale blue, the corp's cobalt family (planned/007, scrambled/007).
   - BATTERED is orange, the same as the ENFORCER type and the Heat bar.
   - DAMAGED (breach/007, breach/017) and BREACHED (breach/027) are both red. The campaign-ending result has no distinct shape or hue in the panel tags; only the HOME pips and the "campaign lost" score text change.
   - Scrambled, the scale cannot be learned (scrambled/007, /017, /027 never show red).
3. **The board does not show the forecast's facts.**
   - The forecast's hits, blocks and HOME numbers exist only as panel text (planned/002, breach/002, breach/022).
   - The board draws candidate routes as dashed lines but puts no outcome marker at the cells that will be hit or blocked.
   - The impact moments are buried in bloom: planned/006, planned/016, scrambled/006 and scrambled/016 show large white glows exactly where the collision should be read.
   - The orange street bars share the ENFORCER hue, and the orange enforcer beams in planned/023 and breach/013 blend into them.

### P2

1. **The hunter is the weakest-reading element, against the README's own claim.**
   - It appears only as a text row ("the street of the first touch," planned/001, breach/001).
   - There is no pre-launch path on the board, and its result surfaces only in the aftermath text ("HUNTER: SOU 1 hit," breach/007).
   - The typed hunter (▲ DRONE in planned/001, ■ NETRUNNER in planned/011) is a nice rule that the board never shows.
2. **The intensity preview ignores the ghost's Heat.**
   - planned/019 shows LOW 61 / NORMAL 67 / HOT 73, each labeled "next: 2 known beams."
   - planned/021 then adds "ghost +5" for 66.
   - Had HOT been picked, 73 + 5 = 78 would cross the 75 tick to 3 known beams, contradicting the card. This is a small prediction lie on the axis the mode is best at.
3. **The careless line got no plan-side guidance.**
   - breach/002 already shows every route DAMAGED on both HOLD and BEST.
   - breach/021 still has GHOST 2 / SPOOF 2 unspent.
   - The forecast only ranks responses, never plan fixes (ghost on HOME, moving a sentry).
   - LAUNCH looks identical whether the forecast shows HOLDS or BREACHED (breach/022 vs planned/022).
   - When all of breach/025's 7 rows say BREACHED, the menu is a dead list.
4. **Build-sentry and territory loss go unexercised.**
   - BUILD ONE SENTRY (-4) is never taken across planned/009, /019, breach/009, /019 and scrambled/009, /019, despite banks of 10–26. It looks dominated, or the 1/1/1 sentry set is too comfortable.
   - Every record says "TAKEN by the corp: 0" (planned/028, breach/028, scrambled/028), so the TAKEN legend item never appears in any capture.
   - A consequence the mode advertises is untested.

### P3

1. **The board is small in the frame.**
   - It occupies about a third of the frame, while the panel and the 10-icon legend take the rest.
   - The row and column labels (NOR/MID/SOU, 0–3) are nearly invisible, yet every text line speaks in those coordinates ("street 3 down," "SOU 0 > MID 1").
2. **The top-bar SCORE jumps within one raid.**
   - planned/000 shows 20, planned/001 shows 38 and planned/007 shows 33. breach/018 shows -2.
   - It is a projection, but it reads as a live score, so it looks erratic.
3. **EVADED appears once without explanation.** It shows up in planned/002 (MID BEST, "untouched"), and its rank relative to HOLDS is unclear.
4. **The yellow-green redeploy target discs are clear** (planned/015, breach/015) and worth keeping.
5. **The "-1" HOME callout is the best single visual in the lab.** It shows as a red outline plus a floating "-1" in breach/006–027.

## Three changes that would raise the score most

1. **Make the reveal matter (D +1.5, S +2, P -0.5).**
   - Keep the per-route HOLD outcome in the forecast, but drop the BEST row, or show it only after the reveal.
   - Have the raid generator guarantee at least one "split" route per raid, where responses differ, so the sealed reveal decides something.
   - Collapse response lists whose options all share an outcome.
2. **Put the forecast on the board (N +2, V +1, W +0.5).**
   - Mark predicted hit, block and HOME-hit cells with shape-coded glyphs, for example shield, crack and skull. Draw the hunter's pre-launch path as a dashed line in the hunter's type color.
   - Recolor the street bars to neutral grey, and move HOLDS off cobalt to the amber "ours" family.
   - Give BREACHED its own shape and hue apart from DAMAGED.
   - Cap bloom on impact frames.
3. **Make the economy bite across the leg (C +1, D +1, P +0.5).**
   - Unrepaired DOWN districts become TAKEN at the next raid, so the corp visibly claws back territory.
   - Price sentries so a 4th sentry is a real alternative to repair and to running LOW.
   - Have the intensity cards include the ghost and spoof Heat in their "next: N known beams" preview.

Together these land around 38–39/50, with S around 7.

## Ceiling note

**The rules are already near the top on P and solid on C. The next ~5 points are a presentation ceiling.** Board-native outcome glyphs, a drawn hunter path, hue separation and a distinct BREACHED state are all things the forecast already knows, so no rules change is needed.

**Beyond roughly 38–39 the ceiling is rules.**
- Three routes, one fixed sentry of each type, and a response menu the forecast can rank exhaustively leave too few interacting choices to reach ITB-level D.
- ITB's depth comes from many units, each with a positional action, and this lab has three static towers plus one response.

**Fit is also a rules ceiling.** Until sentries, kits or responses come from the deck, the raid stays a minigame beside the deckbuilder.
